Footer · birds on wires (lab)

Pigeons, studied from photos: dark grey with a lighter wing panel, not black. Point near one and it turns to look at you; come closer (or tap, on a phone) and it bursts off, its neighbours follow, the group loops around and lands again, flapping for balance and shuffling up. Left alone they preen, stretch a wing, fluff up and shake, and after a while doze off. Wires now move like heavy cable: a slow, small dip when a bird lands and a slight lift when it leaves, plus a faint sway in the wind. Version 5 adds depth: the scene shifts with the pointer, focus follows what you point at, and you can knock on the pole.

5 · Depth (new)

Wires run from a near pole back to a line of far poles. Moving the pointer shifts near and far by different amounts; focus follows it — near the pole, the pole is sharp and the distance blurs, away from it the distance comes into focus. Click the pole to knock it: the thud runs down every wire and the birds take off in the order it reaches them.

6 · Tracking (new)

A crowded set of wires. When the birds go up, a machine starts watching: boxes find them a moment late, lag behind, double up, lose them at the edge and are left hanging. Where each one took off, a pale frame stays for a few seconds. Tap, or leave it alone for a while.
